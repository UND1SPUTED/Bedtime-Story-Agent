# Sample outputs

These stories were produced by the app itself with `claude-haiku-5-5`, using the web UI at `python app.py`. The text and scores are copied from the app's output. Nothing was edited.

Scores are on a 1 to 5 scale from the judge model. A story passes when every criterion is 4 or higher.

## How these samples are recorded

Every story and every change written in the web app is appended to this file automatically. Each entry starts with a `<!-- story-log: N -->` marker, and the counter in the app counts these markers.

## Suggested next samples

More samples should cover different categories and an edge case. Good requests include a calm bedtime story, an animal story, and an unusual or scary-sounding request, which should be handled gently.

**Story:**

Little Bruin and the Grumpy Turtle

In a green forest, where the tall pines hummed in the breeze, lived a baby bear named Bruin. Bruin had a soft brown coat and a round, rumbly tummy. But Bruin often felt lonely. The other animals were busy playing, and Bruin did not know how to join in.

One sunny morning, Bruin wandered down to the quiet pond. There sat an old turtle named Tobias. His shell was dusty and his face looked very grumpy.

"Go away," Tobias grumbled. "I am not in the mood for company."

Bruin's ears drooped. But Bruin wanted a friend very much. So Bruin tried something new. Bruin brought Tobias a juicy blackberry, picked fresh from the bush.

"Hmph," said Tobias. "Berries are fine, I suppose." He did not smile, but he did not shoo Bruin away either.

The next day, Bruin tried again. This time Bruin brought a smooth, flat stone for Tobias to sit on. "Sit on this," said Bruin, "so your feet stay out of the cold mud."

"Hmph," said Tobias again. But he sat on the stone anyway.

On the third day, Bruin felt sad and sat down by the pond. Bruin began to tell a story about the day's worries. Bruin talked about how the other cubs did not wait for him, and how the rain had washed away the sand castle he had built. Bruin's voice wobbled, but Tobias did not interrupt. Tobias just listened, slowly blinking his old eyes.

When Bruin finished, Tobias was quiet for a long time. Then he said softly, "You know, Bruin, I was grumpy because I was lonely too. Nobody ever stops to listen to an old turtle."

Bruin giggled. "I like listening to you, Tobias."

From that day on, Bruin visited the pond every afternoon. Tobias told tales of the river, and Bruin shared stories of the forest. The grumpy old turtle still grumbled sometimes, but now his grumbles came with a twinkle in his eye.

As the sun sank behind the pines, Bruin curled up on the soft moss. The frogs sang their gentle songs, and Tobias hummed along. Bruin closed sleepy eyes, feeling warm and safe, knowing a friend was always waiting by the pond.

Goodnight, little Bruin. Sleep well.

## Sample 2: A small dragon who is afraid of the dark and lights a tiny lantern ... (first draft)

**Request:** A small dragon who is afraid of the dark and lights a tiny lantern with a yawn.

**Judge scores:** Age fit 5/5, Follows the request 4/5, Story arc 5/5, Engagement 4/5, Bedtime calm 5/5, Safety 5/5

**Verdict:** Every criterion met the bar of 4 or higher.

**Story:**

Little Ember and the Yawning Lantern

Ember was a small dragon with soft green scales and a tail that curled like a question mark. Ember lived in a deep, dark cave at the edge of a quiet hill. Ember had one wish. Ember wanted to stop being afraid of the dark.

Every night, when the moon rose, the cave grew very dark. Ember could not see the walls. Ember could not see the pebbles on the floor. Shadows looked like giant shapes, and every drip of water sounded like a tiny footstep.

"Maybe I can make a light," Ember said bravely. Ember puffed out a breath. A puff of smoke came out, but no light. "Oops," said Ember. "That is too much fire. It might tickle the bats."

So Ember tried again. Ember blew very gently, like blowing on a dandelion. A small spark popped, then fizzled out. "Too small," Ember sighed. "I need something in between."

Ember sat down and thought hard. Then Ember looked at a little brass lantern hanging on a hook. It had been a gift from an old owl who visited sometimes. Ember had never used it before.

Ember nudged the lantern with its nose. Ember took a slow, careful breath and let out a warm, steady glow, just the size of a plum. The glow flickered on the lantern's wick. Then something surprising happened.

Ember let out an enormous yawn.

The yawn was so wide and so sleepy that the lantern gave a happy little click. The wick caught the warm air from Ember's mouth, and a soft golden light filled the cave. It was not too bright and not too hot. It was just right.

Ember blinked. The walls were not monsters after all. They were only rocks, smooth and friendly. The pebbles were only pebbles. The drips were only water, sliding home to a puddle.

"Oh," whispered Ember. "The dark is only a quiet time, not a scary time."

Ember curled up beside the lantern. The golden light made the cave feel like a cozy blanket. Ember yawned once more, a little smaller this time.

"Goodnight, lantern," Ember murmured. "Goodnight, cave. Goodnight, bats."

The lantern glowed softly, and Ember's eyes grew heavy. Outside, the moon kept watch over the hill. Inside, a small dragon fell fast asleep, warm and safe, dreaming of stars.

## Sample 3: A boy who helps his grandma bake cookies on a rainy Saturday, and t... (first draft)

**Request:** A boy who helps his grandma bake cookies on a rainy Saturday, and they count the raindrops together.

**Judge scores:** Age fit 5/5, Follows the request 5/5, Story arc 4/5, Engagement 4/5, Bedtime calm 5/5, Safety 5/5

**Verdict:** Every criterion met the bar of 4 or higher.

**Story:**

Rainy Saturday Cookies

It was a rainy Saturday, and rain tapped softly on the kitchen window. Milo wanted to do something special. He wanted to help Grandma bake cookies, and he wanted to be the best cookie helper in the whole house.

Grandma tied an apron around his middle. The apron was so big that it dragged on the floor. "We will fix that," said Grandma with a smile. She rolled up the bottom edge, and Milo stood up tall.

"Can I crack the eggs?" Milo asked.

"You may," said Grandma. "Gently, like you are tapping a little door."

Milo tapped. Crack! A bit of shell fell into the bowl. Milo frowned. Grandma did not scold him. She showed him how to pick the tiny piece out with a spoon. "Mistakes are just part of baking," she said. Milo felt better.

Next, they measured flour and sugar. Milo poured slowly, and a little white cloud floated up into the air. It made them both giggle.

While the dough chilled, the rain grew louder. Plink. Plop. Tap-tap-tap. Milo pressed his nose to the window. "Grandma, how many raindrops do you think are on this window?"

"Let's count together," said Grandma. "You start on the left, and I will start on the right."

They counted slowly. One, two, three. Milo lost his place at twelve, so Grandma said, "Start again, and we will be patient." At last they reached twenty. Milo clapped his hands. "Twenty raindrops, and there are more coming!"

Then the oven timer dinged. The kitchen filled with the warm smell of butter and sugar. Milo and Grandma each took one cookie, still warm and soft. It was the best cookie Milo had ever tasted.

Outside, the rain kept tapping. Milo yawned and leaned against Grandma's arm. "Tomorrow, can we count the raindrops again?"

"Yes, my dear," whispered Grandma. "Rain always comes back."

Milo smiled, and his eyes grew heavy. The raindrops kept time, soft and slow, until he drifted off to sleep.

## Sample 4: Two siblings build a paper rocket in the backyard and fly to the mo... (first draft)

**Request:** Two siblings build a paper rocket in the backyard and fly to the moon for one quiet hour before dinner.

**Judge scores:** Age fit 5/5, Follows the request 5/5, Story arc 5/5, Engagement 4/5, Bedtime calm 5/5, Safety 5/5

**Verdict:** Every criterion met the bar of 4 or higher.

**Story:**

Ten Steps to the Moon

Mia and Leo had one quiet hour before dinner, and they wanted to spend it in space.

"Let's build a rocket," said Mia. She was eight and loved plans.

"A paper one," said Leo, who was six and loved anything that could fly.

They sat on the back step with a stack of old newspaper, a roll of tape, and a bright red crayon. Mia folded a long, pointy nose. Leo folded two wings that stuck out like ears.

They carried the rocket across the grass to the launch pad, which was the flat part of the sandbox. Mia held it up high. Leo blew hard.

The rocket flopped over and landed in the dirt.

"Too floppy," said Mia. She taped the wings tighter.

This time Leo pushed it off the top of the slide. It spun in a circle, bumped the fence, and landed in the clothesline's shadow.

"Too wobbly," said Leo, rubbing his nose.

They sat down. The sun was getting low. Over the fence, a pale circle was rising in the sky.

"That's the moon," whispered Leo. "It's waiting for us."

Mia looked at the rocket. Then she looked at the apple tree. "What if the rocket doesn't need to fly alone? What if we lift it?"

They tied a string around its middle and ran with it, slowly, down the path to the apple tree. The paper rocket rose like a kite, up past the leaves, into the cool evening air.

"We're flying!" shouted Leo.

They floated over the rooftops, past the tall pine, and up to the moon. It was round and soft and glowing, with dusty gray hills and deep, quiet craters. Leo found a crater shaped like a smile. Mia spotted a flag that was only a tiny speck, left by no one at all.

They sat on the moon and watched the stars blink on, one by one. Time seemed to slow down, the way it does on a blanket in summer.

Then, from far away, a voice called, "Dinner!"

Mia and Leo waved goodbye to the moon. They floated gently back down, past the apple tree, and landed softly in the grass.

"Same time tomorrow?" asked Leo, yawning.

"Same time tomorrow," said Mia, and she tucked the rocket under her arm as they walked inside.

## Sample 5: A monster under the bed who is too shy to come out, so a girl teach... (first draft)

**Request:** A monster under the bed who is too shy to come out, so a girl teaches it how to be brave.

**Judge scores:** Age fit 5/5, Follows the request 5/5, Story arc 4/5, Engagement 4/5, Bedtime calm 5/5, Safety 5/5

**Verdict:** Every criterion met the bar of 4 or higher.

**Story:**

The Shy Monster Under the Bed

Mia had a bedroom with a blue rug, a round window, and a bed with a patchwork quilt. Every night she heard a small sniffle coming from underneath it.

One evening, Mia knelt on the rug and peeked under the bed. Two big yellow eyes blinked back at her.

"Hello," said Mia softly. "Who are you?"

"I am Figgle," whispered the monster. He was furry and grey, about the size of a loaf of bread. "I am a monster. But I am too shy to come out."

Mia smiled. "Why are you shy?"

Figgle hid his face behind one paw. "Monsters are supposed to be brave. I am scared of everything. I am scared of the dark, and the creaky stairs, and the very small mouse in the kitchen."

"Then let's practice being brave," said Mia. "Together."

First, they tried a small step. Mia put her pillow on the edge of the bed. "Just stick out your nose," she said. Figgle poked out one tiny nose. Then he pulled it back in quickly. "That was very brave," Mia said, and Figgle wiggled with pride.

Next, they tried a bigger step. Mia held out her hand. "Take one step onto the rug." Figgle wobbled out one furry foot. Then he gasped and scurried back under the bed. "Almost!" Mia giggled. "Let's try again."

Then Figgle had a clever idea. He took a deep breath, the way Mia did before swimming lessons. "Breathe in slowly," Mia reminded him. "Then breathe out, and count to three."

Figgle breathed in. He breathed out. He counted, "One, two, three!" Then he rolled out from under the bed and sat right on the blue rug, blinking in the moonlight.

He looked around the bedroom. The round window glowed silver. The quilt looked cozy. He wasn't scared at all.

"I did it!" Figgle cried happily. "I came out!"

"You did," said Mia. "You are very brave, Figgle."

From that night on, Figgle still liked to tuck himself under the bed at bedtime. But now he could come out whenever he wanted. Sometimes he and Mia sat by the window and counted stars together.

Now it was time for sleep. Mia yawned. Figgle yawned too. "Goodnight, Mia," he whispered.

"Goodnight, Figgle," said Mia, and she turned off the lamp. The room grew soft and dim, and both of them drifted off to sleep.
